\begin{abstract}
The cost of geometric algebra computation depends on the requested output,
operand structure, metric, reuse horizon, and execution device, as well as
ambient dimension. We examine the design of the Garamon C++ generator and
organize a set of exact and explicitly approximate strategies for
Garamon.jl. The strategies draw on sparse joins, recursive traversal,
preparation and caching, compact representations, algebraic structure,
and parallel execution. For each family we state the mathematical
precondition, the work it may avoid, and the costs that a comparison must
include. A DrWatson-based protocol specifies common workloads, independent
correctness checks, matched C++/Julia comparisons, profiling, and resumable
measurements across machines. Available performance panels show qualified
same-input warm-time ratios; validation-only and empty slots identify work
without such measurements. Full episode, memory, and cross-library results
remain separate requirements. No general speed ranking or learned selector
is inferred here.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
An algebra \(\Cl(V,G)\) with \(n=\dim V\) has \(2^n\) basis blades, but a
particular request need not materialize all their coefficients. A product
of a few active blades, a selected scalar coefficient, a repeated
homogeneous product, and a complete dense product are different
computational tasks. A method suited to one task may spend most of its
time converting data or preparing a plan for another. The research
question is therefore which exact calculation to perform for a
specified workload, and which preparation can be amortized over its
future use.

Garamon C++ generates an algebra-specific library from a metric and
configuration~\cite{breuils2019,cppmain}. Garamon.jl is a Julia
implementation in which algebra and operand structure can also be
handled at run time~\cite{garamonjulia}. This distinction makes
preparation, specialization, ownership of temporary memory, and the
definition of an equivalent comparison central to the study. The
present manuscript describes the candidate techniques and their
experimental contracts. It excludes exploratory timings from earlier
local runs. Qualified partial results are shown where available; each
caption separates the metric actually plotted from the fuller comparison
planned for that technique.

\section{Algebraic and numerical contract}
Let \(e_A\) denote the ordered blade indexed by a bit mask \(A\).
For an orthogonal basis with \(e_i^2=g_i\),
\begin{equation}
 e_Ae_B=
 \sigma(A,B)\left(\prod_{i\in A\cap B}g_i\right)e_{A\triangle B},
 \qquad \sigma(A,B)\in\{-1,+1\}.
 \label{eq:blade}
\end{equation}
The symmetric difference \(A\triangle B\) identifies the output mask;
the sign counts swaps needed to restore basis order. Thus a nonzero
term of a bilinear product can be found by enumerating operand pairs,
or by solving for one operand mask when an output mask is requested.
The latter uses \(B=A\triangle K\) for a requested \(K\), provided
the product and basis satisfy the assumptions of
Equation~\eqref{eq:blade}. For a coefficient of \((ab)c\), the
corresponding mask condition is
\(C=A\triangle B\triangle K\); the scalar factors and signs must still
be evaluated in the original multiplication order.

We call a product \emph{algorithmically exact} when no mathematically
nonzero contribution to its declared output is intentionally omitted.
This term does not promise bit-identical floating-point sums:
association, reduction order, and basis transformations can affect
rounding. Every experiment must state whether it requires equality
over an exact coefficient domain, a bound on floating-point error, or
a specified accumulation order. A geometric product, a scalar
coefficient, a set of grades, a streamed sequence of coefficients,
and a sign predicate have different output contracts. A shortcut
valid for one does not automatically implement another.

For a general symmetric metric, off-diagonal contractions contribute
additional terms. A diagonal-basis XOR kernel is admissible only after
a valid basis transformation or under a diagonal metric. The
transformation itself can expand supports and introduce numerical
error; its preparation and application belong to the timed episode.
Degenerate metrics need additional care because diagonalization,
duality, and inversion need not have the same contracts as in a
nondegenerate algebra.

\section{Code-grounded starting point}
\subsection{Garamon C++}
The audited Garamon C++ revision reads a generator configuration,
constructs blade indexing data, handles the metric and changes of
basis, and emits C++ product code~\cite{cppmain,cppindex,cppmetric}.
Its multivector representation records present grades and stores a
dense coefficient vector inside each present grade~\cite{cppmvec}.
Product emission can unroll selected cases; other cases traverse a
virtual prefix tree recursively~\cite{cppdispatch,cppgeometric}. A
virtual tree is a traversal scheme, not evidence that coefficient
nodes or intermediate subproducts are materialized and memoized.
Generated code, transform matrices, and dense grade blocks are
preparation choices whose cost depends on the algebra and reuse
horizon.

This audit describes the cited revision, rather than every possible
Garamon C++ fork. An optimization to its Eigen temporaries, for
example, would require a separate source change and a matched
validation before it could enter a language comparison. Merely
comparing the generator's public multivector call with a Julia
packed-array kernel would mix representation and algorithm effects.

\subsection{Garamon.jl and its benchmark harness}
The Julia package exposes algebra definitions, mask indexing,
dense and sparse multivectors, direct and targeted products,
prepared plans, an expression route, generated kernels, workspaces,
and selected structured prototypes~\cite{garamonjulia}. The
implementation is Julia code, not a wrapper around the C++ library.
These paths have different admission conditions and output
ownership. Their existence in source is separate from a performance
claim or qualification for an arbitrary metric and dimension.

GaramonBench.jl carries configurations, adapters, the technique
inventory, oracle preflights, provenance, and resumable campaign
execution~\cite{garamonbench,drwatson}. The compatibility matrix in
\path{config/compatibility_matrix.csv} records proposed pairwise
compositions; \path{config/techniques.toml} gives their conditions.
A matrix entry is a mathematical planning hypothesis, not a measured
speedup. The benchmark article is under \path{papers/}; raw data,
generated plots, and PDFs are excluded from Git. Qualified article
tables and their provenance can be committed under
\path{data/processed/garamonbench/}.

All routes within a paired comparison receive the same seeded
mathematical inputs. The seed controls scenario generation, not
merely plot styling. Support topology, coefficients, metric,
precision, output selection, and recurrence feedback are recorded
per case. A complete output may be compared with a selected output
only as two different tasks, not as an algorithmic speed ratio.

The small dense scenario requests the full product in dimensions where
all coefficients can fit in memory. Its purpose is to expose indexing,
allocation, and output-writing costs rather than to reward a method for
omitting requested coefficients. The sparse ambient scenario keeps the
number of active blades small while varying ambient dimension and the
distance between their indices. It tests whether a method pays for the
ambient algebra or only for the active support. Homogeneous and targeted
requests are separate scenarios because their output contracts differ;
each is paired only with a standard route that returns the same requested
quantity.

Reuse scenarios range from one call to long episodes with fixed metric
and supports but changing coefficients. Another episode alternates
support topologies or algebras. The first tests amortization of plans,
workspaces, generated code, and cached indices. The second tests
invalidation, cache admission, and the cost of preparing a route that
may never be reused. Recurrences add feedback between calls and require
the output of one step to become the exact input of the next; replay and
approximate methods therefore receive their own error contract.

In the figure legends, \emph{horizon} is the number of product or
operation requests in the measured episode: horizon 1 is one request,
while horizon 32 is a sequence of 32 requests under the scenario's
reuse or change rules. It is not the algebra dimension. \emph{Signature}
names the metric's sign and degeneracy pattern, such as positive,
mixed, or degenerate;
\emph{support} describes which blade coefficients are nonzero.
The grade-pair labels \code{one\_one} and \code{top\_one} mean two
grade-one operands and a top-grade/grade-one pair, respectively.
In cache figures, \emph{trace} is the order of requested support
contexts: \code{scan} cycles through four contexts, \code{phase}
groups requests into four phases, and \code{shift} changes context
every two requests. Each plotted ratio uses the standard Garamon.jl
route on the same generated inputs. A ratio below one favors the
candidate on warm time only; the black line marks equality. A red
ten-times line appears only when at least one measured case crosses
that local review threshold.

Metric scenarios distinguish positive diagonal, mixed signature,
degenerate, and general symmetric forms. An algorithm specialized to a
diagonal metric must include any change-of-basis and reconstruction
cost when compared with a direct general-metric calculation. Parallel
scenarios vary batch length, CPU thread or process count, GPU residency,
transfer frequency, and output ownership. The complete episode and the
warm kernel are reported separately so that an advantage in one is not
silently attributed to the other.

For every comparable strategy, the campaign must measure a standard
Garamon.jl route on exactly the same generated requests and checks both
outputs. A second reference uses the generated Garamon C++ library on
the same requests whenever the campaign can match the dimension, metric,
arithmetic, operation, and output coefficients. Each matched C++ result
passes the same independent coefficient check. The compiler and native
operand preparation run before warm timing. The warm C++ measurement
includes one call from Julia per batch and returns an owned result.
The current build budget covers at most \(2^{20}\) basis masks. Above
dimension 8, the generated library uses recursive products for grade
blocks with more than 32 coefficients. Degenerate metrics use recursive
products throughout to avoid missing generated functions for vanishing
grade blocks. A ratio requires a qualified timing for its corresponding
reference.
Alternative complete execution routes may share one figure and one
baseline; composable techniques are evaluated in separate interaction
experiments rather than presented as mutually exclusive choices.
After a paired pilot, a route that remains roughly an order of magnitude
slower than the standard route may stop its sweep within that workload
regime. The threshold and all censored cases are recorded. This local
stopping decision does not exclude the route at other dimensions, reuse
horizons, output contracts, or devices.

\section{Representation, indexing, and locality}
\paragraph{How to read a measured panel.}
The horizontal coordinate is the number of basis directions \(n\), not
the number of nonzero coefficients. A point is one completed parameter
combination, checked against an independent exact oracle before timing.
Its vertical coordinate is the median warm execution time of the named
route divided by the median warm time of its own same-input standard
Garamon.jl route. When available, the lower panel uses the generated
Garamon C++ reference for the same case. The markers and colors identify
the same scenarios in both panels. The black dashed line marks equal
time, and values below one favor the named route. The vertical scale follows
the completed ratios; it is not fixed to a remote value such as
\(10^{-4}\), and measured points are not clipped to a threshold.
When a case exceeds ten times the baseline, a red dotted line at ten
shows the campaign's local slow-route review threshold, not an error
bound or a second library. A red cross at the chart top denotes a
resource exclusion with no timing result.
Color and marker shape encode only the categories explicitly named in
each caption. Different parameter combinations can share a color and
even the same plotted position; they are not averaged into a new
algorithm. ``Horizon'' \(H=1\) means one product request in an episode;
\(H=32\) means 32 successive requests in the specified workload
sequence, usually with fresh coefficients. A warm ratio alone does not
charge one-time plan construction, retained memory, compilation, or the complete
episode. The metric label \emph{positive} means all diagonal basis
squares are positive; \emph{mixed} alternates positive and negative
squares; \emph{degenerate} also sets every third diagonal square to
zero. This is the metric's sign pattern, not a sign imposed on the
random input coefficients. Separately, reversing the order of two
basis directions contributes a minus sign to a blade product; repeated
directions contribute their metric diagonal value and can make a term
zero in a degenerate metric. Thus the sign of an output coefficient
combines blade-order parity, metric factors, and input values. The
dimensions 64/65 and 128/129 deliberately cross blade-mask storage
boundaries where the implementation changes integer representation;
they are not separate algorithms. The following guides repeat these facts
where each comparison needs them; the page link in every caption
returns here or to its technique-specific guide.
\subsection{Dense, sparse, and grade-block forms}
A dense multivector stores an addressable coefficient for each blade
in its admitted algebra. Its simple indexing can be attractive when
most of the output is required and materialized. Its storage grows
as \(2^n\), so the memory required by the output alone can rule it
out. A sparse dictionary or sorted mask list stores only active
blades but pays for lookup, insertion, ordering, and allocation.
Grade blocks exploit known homogeneous structure: grade \(r\)
contains \(\binom nr\) possible blades, yet actual occupancy can
vary widely. A benchmark must include conversion between forms,
the structure of the result, and whether the caller owns or borrows
the output. Work on sparse tensor workspaces and compiler-generated
sparse iteration motivates this representation-aware comparison
without supplying a geometric-algebra speed ranking~\cite{taco,sparseworkspace,finch}.
The three representation slots test dense storage
(Figure~\ref{fig:technique-01}), sparse supports
(Figure~\ref{fig:technique-02}), and grade blocks
(Figure~\ref{fig:technique-03}) against their respective same-input
standard routes. The first and third panels vary input structure;
the second is a direct-route reference check, as explained below.

\subsection{Factorization, active subspaces, and compact diagrams}
A decomposable blade and a product of versors can remain factorized
while transformations are applied. Expanding them into a general
multivector is an explicit conversion, not a free consequence of
factorization. If all operands live in a certified coordinate
subspace, the calculation can be performed with fewer active
directions and mapped back. A general linear subspace needs a valid
change of basis and must account for its conditioning and support
expansion.

The registered factored-blade campaign transforms a decomposable bivector
with one to eight nonnull reflections, over repeated episodes and seeded
coefficient signs. Every grade-two coefficient is requested. Its oracle
uses coordinate reflections and minors; the ordinary-product baseline
expands the blade and performs the complete ordered sandwich. Intermediate
grades have an explicit expansion budget and are never discarded.
Figures~\ref{fig:technique-17} and \ref{fig:technique-18} locate the
factored-versor and certified active-subspace experiments. They ask
whether saved expansion or ambient work repays certification and
conversion over the specified request sequence.

Tensor trains represent coefficient tensors with cores; they are

\subsection{Binary rank and invariant sectors}
Let \(S\) be the union of masks relevant to a diagonal-metric
request, viewed as vectors over \(\mathbb F_2\). If the span has rank
\(d\), XOR combinations lie in a space of at most \(2^d\) masks.
This is an indexing opportunity, not a claim that the product has
become the standard Clifford algebra in dimension \(d\).
An embedding \(\phi:\mathbb F_2^d\to\mathbb F_2^n\) must carry
the multiplication sign, metric cocycle, ambient grade, and output
queries. A basis of masks can have commuting or nilpotent generators
that would be misrepresented by a naive compressed metric.

The first implemented invariant-sector case is the eigenspace split of
the grade involution \(\alpha(X)=\sum_k(-1)^k\langle X\rangle_k\).
Writing \(X=X_++X_-\) for even and odd grades, the involution's
automorphism property gives
\(\langle AB\rangle_{\mathrm{even}}=A_+B_++A_-B_-\) and
\(\langle AB\rangle_{\mathrm{odd}}=A_+B_-+A_-B_+\).
Thus a requested parity uses two sector products, whereas a general
product recomposes all four. This exact decomposition is distinct from
discarding small intermediate coefficients; every contribution to the
requested output remains present. The current implementation prepares
the four structural plans for a repeated integer, diagonal-metric
request, checks metric, basis and input supports on each call, and
falls back to the full exact product when a certificate is invalid.
The preparation and four-plan memory cost may outweigh the saved pair
products, especially for short horizons. The campaign varies parity,
dimension, signature, support, reuse horizon and invalidation, and
compares prepared sectors with the direct exact route under the same
independent signed-blade oracle. The underlying grading is standard
Clifford-algebra structure~\cite{breuils2019}; no performance conclusion
is drawn before the dedicated-machine measurements.
The compact-mask rank test (Figure~\ref{fig:technique-31}), exact
sector split (Figure~\ref{fig:technique-32}), and combined rank/workspace
episode (Figure~\ref{fig:technique-K1}) have separate result slots
because their certificates and retained storage differ.

\subsection{Expression sharing and algebraic rewriting}
An expression DAG can share a subexpression within one evaluation
when purity and dependencies are known. Memoizing its numeric value
across evaluations additionally requires invalidation when input
coefficients change. Equality saturation~\cite{egg} can retain several
equivalent representations before choosing an evaluation order. Our
bounded implementation uses a restricted e-graph: each interval of a
noncommutative product chain is an equivalence class containing every
contiguous binary split. Associativity makes these splits equivalent
for exact Clifford arithmetic; the implementation does not rewrite
factor order or apply floating-point associativity. A dynamic program
extracts the split with the smallest conservative sum of support-pair
counts. Its support sets include every blade mask reachable under a
diagonal integral metric, disregarding possible numeric cancellation.
The resulting plan accepts coefficient updates only while supports
and the metric remain fixed. Candidate and support budgets refuse
unbounded preparation rather than silently dropping terms.

The campaign varies ten dimensions, factor count, support width and
change pattern, plan preparation, and episode horizon. It compares
the complete accumulated output with the ordinary left-associated
Garamon.jl product chain on identical seeded inputs. Preparation,
evaluation, retained plan storage, and full-episode cost are separate
observations; identical baseline scenarios reuse their archived
baseline measurement. An independent exact blade-word enumeration
checks the output before timing. Figure~\ref{fig:technique-23}
remains provisional until isolated measurements qualify.
Figures~\ref{fig:technique-16} and \ref{fig:technique-23}
cover expression sharing and bounded associative rewriting,

cannot promise that an owned complete output smaller than its
information content will fit in memory. The budget includes input,
output, plans, compiled code, device buffers, and runtime overhead.
An admission failure must be explicit. This route is compared on
time, peak memory, page faults, and completeness of the emitted
coefficient stream, not only on inner-kernel allocation counts.

\subsection{Structural caches}
A plan cache stores topology-dependent work, not mutable coefficient
values. LRU, roulette eviction, TinyLFU admission, and SIEVE are
alternative policies for retaining plans under a bounded
memory budget~\cite{tinylfu,sieve}. Roulette eviction is exact:
randomness changes which plan will be rebuilt, not which product
contributions are evaluated. Its retention probabilities are
benchmark parameters. A policy must be compared on hit rate,
rebuild time, memory, metadata cost, and workload sequence; a
policy that helps repeated stable supports can hurt a rapidly
changing algebra. Two replacement policies for the same cache slot
are alternatives unless a new, explicitly specified cache
hierarchy composes them.

\subsection{JIT generation, precompilation, and restart}
On-demand code generation may unroll a bounded path set for a
known support pattern. Generated code size, compilation latency,
and invalidation are part of its cost. The generated arithmetic uses
typed operand and output buffers; allocation and the runtime output
size remain outside the emitted path sequence. This keeps the bounded
kernel inferable without adding a specialization for each output size.
A catalogue for frequently
used algebras can shift some compilation before a session, but
machine architecture, Julia version, metric, scalar type, and
kernel source must be included in its identity. The code loaded
after restart is a different resource from an in-process warm
method~\cite{juliaprecompile,fftw}. Tests therefore distinguish
source preprocessing, JIT compilation, catalogue construction,
reload, and repeated execution. No catalogue is presumed optimal
for users who cannot predict their future algebra.
The catalogue build uses the campaign's pinned dependency closure,
including when the diagnostic worker itself has a smaller environment.
Disk-cache integrity is checked during verification and cleanup, outside
the timed product call. Fresh-state diagnostics include repeated native
catalogue construction in their setup budget.

requests reuse that known algebra, introduce an untrained four-dimensional
metric and support, or alternate several untrained contexts. Request

\subsection{Degeneracy and short identities}
For a degenerate metric, the radical is the kernel of its bilinear
form. A certified radical decomposition can expose a nilpotent
filtration, permitting a finite correction around an invertible
quotient component. An isotropic vector alone does not certify
membership in the radical. The calculation must preserve
multiplication order and the inverse's domain; failed
certification triggers a general route or an explicit rejection.

The inverse campaign varies radical size, active nonradical coordinates,
coefficient phase and ambient dimension. The exact oracle solves the full
active left-regular system by independent Gauss--Jordan elimination.
Ordinary Garamon.jl general inversion supports at most five coordinates;
larger ambient cases therefore use that ordinary inverse in the exactly
isomorphic active coordinate subalgebra and embed its complete answer
back. This baseline scope must accompany any reported timing.

certificate and exact fallback experiment; a refused certificate
remains an observed outcome rather than a missing timing point.

A cross-Gram matrix addresses the scalar pairing of two
decomposable, equal-grade blades, rather than all coefficients of a
general geometric product. Let
\(A=u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge u_k\) and
\(B=v_1\wedge\cdots\wedge v_k\). The exterior-power pairing obeys
\(\langle\widetilde A B\rangle_0=\det G\), where
\(G_{ij}=\langle u_i,v_j\rangle\). Consequently
\(\operatorname{rank}G<k\) is an exact zero certificate for this
particular scalar request. The implemented route forms the contractions
with arbitrary-precision integer arithmetic, computes rank and
determinant by exact elimination, and stores the vectors and metric as
the certificate. It checks those inputs on every call; changed vectors
or metric trigger a complete exact recomputation. Scalar rescaling of
the same blades can reuse the saved determinant. Grade is currently
bounded by 16 and the metric is diagonal and integral. The independent
pre-flight oracle evaluates the determinant by a permutation sum for
grades two and three; separate algebra tests compare the result with
the scalar part of the reversed-blade geometric product. The campaign
varies dimension, grade, signature, rank regime, vector support,
mutation and reuse horizon. Its cost accounting includes matrix
construction and certificate validation. No rank claim is extended
to unrelated coefficients or nondecomposable inputs.

The implemented fermionic route is an explicitly restricted,
number-conserving Gaussian subclass. A decomposable \(k\)-particle
Slater state is stored as an \(n\times k\) orbital matrix \(U\).
Each elementary quadratic-generator map
\(\exp(ca_i^\dagger a_j)\), \(i\ne j\), induces the exact one-body
shear \(U_{i,:}\leftarrow U_{i,:}+cU_{j,:}\). A sequence therefore
acts on the orbital matrix without expanding the full Fock-space
amplitude vector. For a target Slater matrix \(V\), the requested
overlap is \(\det(V^\mathsf{T}U')\), with \(U'\) the transformed
orbitals. We compare a factored shear sequence with its materialized
one-body matrix, including the different preparation and memory costs.
The integers make this a generally nonunitary but exact algebraic
subclass; it does not cover pairing terms, arbitrary Gaussian maps,
non-Gaussian states, or general multivectors. Such inputs are rejected
instead of silently projected onto the Slater class. The independent
oracle evaluates the small overlap determinant by a permutation sum,
and algebra tests expand selected Slater states to check the same
scalar pairing. The campaign varies mode count, particle count, shear
count, orbital support, target relation and reuse horizon. The broader
simulation context of noninteracting fermions and Slater states is
established in~\cite{terhal2002,divincenzo2005}; the present exact-integer
shear restriction is our benchmark contract.

\subsection{Exact modular and filtered decisions}
For integer coefficients and a diagonal integer metric, the coefficient
of output mask $k$ has the exact sum
\begin{equation}
c_k=\sum_{a\mathbin{\triangle}b=k}
  \epsilon(a,b)\,x_a y_b\!
  \prod_{i\in a\cap b}g_i,
\qquad
B_k=\sum_{a\mathbin{\triangle}b=k}
  \left|x_a y_b\prod_{i\in a\cap b}g_i\right|,
\end{equation}
where $\epsilon$ is the blade permutation sign and $B_k$ bounds
$|c_k|$. The implementation prepares structural paths and small prime
moduli, evaluates the same full sum modulo each prime, and reconstructs
the signed integer by the Chinese remainder theorem only when the product
of the moduli exceeds $2\max_k B_k$. This makes even a reconstructed zero
exact. If the selected prime budget is insufficient, an exact arbitrary
precision accumulation takes over; no contribution is removed.
Multimodular arithmetic is attractive when coefficient size makes native
arbitrary precision multiplication expensive, but reduction, primes,
reconstruction, and the coefficient-bound pass may outweigh it for short
episodes~\cite{vanhoe}.

The campaign varies 17- and 31-bit primes, one to sixteen primes,
coefficient sizes, metric signatures, ten ambient dimensions, and reuse
horizons. It compares a prepared structural plan and a one-shot call with
direct arbitrary precision accumulation under identical seeded inputs;
irrelevant prime settings are omitted from the direct baseline. The
requested output is the complete
integer multivector in both modes; the independent oracle accumulates
signed blade pairs directly. These comparisons establish whether and
where the modular route repays preparation, without claiming a speed
advantage before the dedicated run.

A filtered predicate answers a narrower question than the complete
product: the exact sign of one requested coefficient. For integer
coefficients and a diagonal integer metric, a structural plan joins
only blade pairs whose XOR is the requested mask. The first execution
pass converts every operand and structural factor to 128-bit integers
and uses checked multiplication and addition. If all checks succeed,
the integer sum and its sign are exact. If any conversion or operation
overflows, the entire coefficient is recomputed from the same paths
using arbitrary precision integers. This is an integer analogue of
filtered exact predicates with a certified fallback~\cite{cgalfiltered};
it does not use an unproven floating-point tolerance. Preparation,
checked execution, and fallback cost are measured separately across
ten dimensions, metric signatures, coefficient sizes, cancellation
regimes, and reuse horizons. A direct arbitrary precision coefficient
sum is the same-contract baseline, with identical seeded inputs.
The decision API must not silently replace a request for a full
multivector.

For a repeated three-input chain, a structural certificate can be
propagated through an intermediate product. The first prepared plan
enumerates every potentially nonzero output mask from its operand
supports and metric. Those masks, treated as a support upper bound,
form the left input of the second prepared plan. Numerical cancellation
may remove intermediate masks, but it cannot create a mask outside this
upper bound. The second execution therefore accepts a subset of its
certified input support while evaluating every retained path. Both
plans snapshot the basis, metric and operand supports. A changed input
support or metric invalidates the certificate; the specified fallback
computes the complete chain directly. This extends the idea of
reusable structural work in sparse computation~\cite{sparseworkspace}
to a multi-step algebraic dependency with explicit invalidation.
The campaign compares preparation and repeated execution against the
same direct chain under shared seeds, across ten dimensions, metric
signatures, support sizes and stable, cancelling, support-changing and
metric-changing episodes. It records the number of fallbacks and the
gap between predicted and actual intermediate support.

\section{Approximation is a separate contract}
Contribution thresholds, stochastic contribution roulette, and
learned pruning may save work for an application that explicitly
accepts approximation. They can omit a nonzero term and therefore
cannot implement the exact product defined above. Recursive
structure does not guarantee that an omitted term will
reappear later. An exact replay route can defer work only when it
retains sufficient provenance to reconstruct every contribution
needed by the final request. For approximate recurrences, the
study must report whole-trajectory error, rare events, bias,
random seeds, and the cost of any residual or replay. The
approximate API and its figures remain separate from the exact
comparisons.

\section{Parallel execution and selection}
Threads can partition independent products or output regions, but
must avoid races and declare the order of reduction. Processes can
isolate compilation or exploit separate memory, while paying for
serialization, duplicated plans, and communication~\cite{juliathreads}.
SIMD and packed batches require a layout and homogeneous enough
work to offset packing cost. GPU comparisons distinguish host
inputs from resident inputs, kernel count from complete episodes,
and borrowed device output from owned host output~\cite{cudaperf}.
Kernel fusion and captured graphs are separate candidates for
repeated resident work; their preparation and output semantics
must match the CPU episode being compared.

\section{Combining strategies}
Several techniques act at different stages: a certified active
subspace can precede a targeted join; a prepared join can use a
private workspace; a cache can retain its plan; CPU threads can
evaluate independent requests. Each composition still needs a
joint contract. For example, compressed binary rank changes mask
coordinates and needs the multiplication cocycle transported;
a plan keyed only by compressed dimension is unsafe. A
workspace plus cache must account for both retained allocations.
A GPU kernel may reject a scalar type admitted on CPU. Pairwise
compatibility does not prove that three methods compose.

The package's machine-readable matrix uses \(\mathrm{O}\) for a
composition compatible by construction in a common domain,
\(C_i\) for a stated condition, \(X\) for alternatives occupying
the same resource, and \(?\) when a useful transfer is not yet
demonstrated. It is a design aid. The conditions in
\path{config/techniques.toml} must be checked at run time or by a
static certificate before the corresponding combination is
measured. Exact-mode admission always overrides a heuristic
speed prediction.

\section{Reproducible experimental program}
\subsection{Qualification before timing}
Each route receives a minimal oracle preflight for supported
metrics, masks, grades, scalar types, output forms, and rejection
cases. An independent reference checks every requested
coefficient or exact-domain identity. A prototype can be
correct on its fixture without having a general API; the
manuscript will state that scope. A candidate with no
implementation remains a proposal and receives no performance
point. Only after correctness and output ownership pass do
PerfChecker runs investigate elapsed time, CPU stacks, allocation
stacks, garbage collection, type inference, peak memory, and
compilation. GPU profiling includes transfers, launches, device
memory, synchronization, and instrumented traces. Profiling
overhead is kept out of timing samples.

\subsection{Crossed workload parameters}
The first sweep spans small, intermediate, and sparse
high-dimensional algebras; the later dedicated sweep can proceed
through consecutive dimensions until the declared memory or time
budget rejects a case. Supports include dense, sparse, clustered,
dispersed, homogeneous, and changing patterns. Metrics include
diagonal positive, mixed signature, degenerate, and general
symmetric cases where a route supports them. Requested outputs,
numeric types, reuse horizons, cache budgets, roulette
probabilities, workspace capacities, thread and process counts,
and device availability are varied explicitly. The grid records
unsupported and resource-rejected cases rather than hiding them.

A workload sequence is generated once from a recorded seed and
replayed unchanged for every admissible route. Stable topology
with changing coefficients, repeated identical objects, alternating
contexts, and continually changing algebras are separate
scenarios. Preparation, cold compilation, first invocation, warm
kernel, complete episode, retained memory, and output
materialization have separate fields. An isolated-machine label
must be supplied by the operator and accompanied by machine and
process snapshots; earlier local data under competing workloads
are not mixed with the dedicated campaign.

\subsection{Comparability and publication}
A causal C++/Julia language comparison requires the same strategy,
algebra, input and output layouts, precision rules, accumulation order
where required, and preparation boundary. Standard C++ and Julia routes
can also serve as application-level baselines for a new strategy when
they answer the same request on the same inputs; such a comparison does
not isolate language overhead. Other GA
libraries receive the same mathematical scenario and independent
oracle; their different internal strategies are stated.
Compile flags, Julia flags, bounds-check settings, CPU affinity,
thread counts, GPU driver, and source revisions are recorded.

DrWatson organizes cases, names data and plot locations, and
captures provenance~\cite{drwatson}. A completed case is checked
before it is skipped on restart; interruption does not require
rerunning validated cases. Raw samples and qualification reports
remain distinct from derived CSV summaries and article plots.
The benchmark machine receives the package and starts a fresh
archive. If a performance figure is absent, the manuscript shows a
validation-only preflight card when a matching oracle archive exists,
or an explicit empty slot otherwise. The preflight card reports no time
or ranking and is replaced only by qualified dedicated-machine results.

\section{Discussion and conclusion}
The proposed palette is useful only if admission checks connect
each method to the request it can answer. Ambient dimension
constrains possible storage, but support, grade, output selection,
metric, recurrence, and reuse decide which work can be avoided.
Preprocessing and compilation may be worthwhile over long
episodes, whereas one-off requests can be dominated by setup.
Structural certificates can change the mathematical size of a
special task; they do not justify deleting contributions from a
general exact product. RAM and device capacity are part of
feasibility, not afterthoughts.

This manuscript establishes algorithms, their conditions, and a
reproducible comparison design. Available warm-time panels are partial
evidence and do not establish a general empirical ranking.
Further qualified dedicated-machine data will complete the result slots,
report uncertainty and rejected cases,
and only then evaluate a workload selector and its
hyperparameters.
